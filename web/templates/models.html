How the models work

True value

Every veteran contract on file is a price the market paid for a player. The model looks at the season before each contract was signed (box score, snap counts, age against the position's usual career, experience, draft slot, position, and the season before that) and learns what share of the salary cap that production earned. A share of the cap is used instead of dollars so a 2014 deal and a 2025 deal are comparable; multiplying by today's cap turns it back into today's dollars.

Applied to any player-season, it answers: if this player hit free agency after this season, what would he get? Players on rookie deals are scored as if they were veterans, so their surplus shows how much of a bargain the rookie contract is.

Age and recent seasons

Only recent seasons count. True value uses a player's latest season and the one before it, nothing older. An MVP year five seasons ago adds nothing. (Using only the latest season was tested too: accuracy dropped {% if tv.r2 %}from R² {{ tv.r2 }}{% endif %} to about 0.72 and values swung wildly year to year, so one prior season stays in.)

Age is measured against the position's usual career. A 33-year-old kicker is mid-career; a 33-year-old running back is past the end. For each position, the typical career end is the average age in the final season of players with 4+ season careers that ended 2000–2023. The model is not allowed to value a player higher for having less runway left.

Age adjustment. Old players who still produce are rare, and the ones in the data (Brady, Brees) were exceptional, so stats alone overrate them. Comparing real contracts with stats-only prices shows how much the market discounts players past the usual career end; that curve is applied as the age adjustment.

Next-season projections

A second model predicts a player's true value next season from this season and last season. It is tested with a walk-forward backtest: to predict each season from {{ pr.backtest_seasons.split('-')[0] }} on, it was trained only on earlier seasons, the same way it predicts a real future season. It only learns from players who played at least 4 games the next year, so it predicts how well someone will play, not whether he'll be healthy or on a roster.
